Why we could not simply buy it

This was the first question the board asked, and it is a fair one. We looked at the market properly. The problem is structural, not commercial: the ERP market is organised around single lines of business, and ZOT runs three.

Sector-specific ERPs

Deep and granular for one discipline. Buying three means buying three silos, three integrations and three sets of terminology, which is what we already had.

Multi-division ERPs

Broad enough to cover all three, but shallow. They lose the process detail: plating, etch, impedance, bow and twist. That is exactly where our quality and cost live.

What we needed

Process-level detail per division and a single aggregated view across them. No vendor sells that combination, because almost nobody needs it. We do.

So the decision was not “build rather than buy” on principle. It was that the aggregated view (the thing the directors actually wanted) could only be assembled on top of the granular systems we already ran. Everything after this follows from that.
