01

About Me

I completed my MSc in Artificial Intelligence at the University of Edinburgh, and my BE in Electronics and Communication Engineering at Anna University.

I joined ZOT in February 2025 as a Software Application Engineer intern, and I have since been promoted to Software Engineer. Over a little more than a year and a half, it has been a continuous learning experience: getting to know the business from the ground up, from the work of an operator on the floor through to the priorities of the managing director, and collaborating with knowledgeable colleagues at every level. By combining their deep manufacturing expertise with what I bring on the technology side, we have been able to build and transform the way the business works from within.

I focus on understanding how people actually work, and on designing technology that fits naturally around them, so that the tools genuinely support the team and add lasting value to the business.

02

Architecting the platform

As the company's first software engineer, my role was to push the company's digital frontier forward and bring Industry 4.0 to the factory floor, and every new idea needed a solid platform beneath it. So I built that platform first. I sat with colleagues across every division to understand how the industry and the company actually worked, chose the right technology stack, and architected a framework that tied it all together into a production-grade system.

FEB – MAR 2025

Mapped the three divisional databases, a first for the company. Built the data layer and first dashboards.

APR – AUG 2025

Productionized the website. Division KPI dashboards. Digital quotation replacing spreadsheets. CRM foundations.

OCT – DEC 2025

Cloud estate built with security partners. Site deployed live. Recruitment and payroll automation.

JAN – FEB 2026

Cloud linked to on-site systems. Live production dashboards across all three divisions. Role-based access.

MAR – APR 2026

Vortex customer order portal launched. Customer profiles. Shop-floor sensors connected to the cloud.

MAY – JUN 2026

Automated optical inspection. Digital job cards on the PCB floor.

JUL 2026 – PRESENT

Finance, Work in Progress and Sales dashboards. Digital quotation rolled out across divisions.

15

Wider influence

The part I am most pleased about is not a system. It is that colleagues now bring me ideas. Marketing worked with me on the website. HR asked for the recruitment pipeline and now uses it for everything. Sales use the costed bill of materials for pricing. The job cards started as a conversation with a colleague on the PCB floor, and we took the proposal to the Managing Director together.

Some of it is quieter capability building. The payroll rules and the database map existed only in one or two people's heads. Writing them down and putting them in a system means the company owns that knowledge now, and it does not leave when someone does.

Outside the business, ZOT encouraged me to attend research conferences and technical meetups, where I could learn from engineers with far more experience than me. My placement was published as a case study by the Midlothian and East Lothian Chamber of Commerce, and I am glad it is being used to make the case for taking on technical interns in manufacturing. That route is how I got here.

16

What comes next

The foundation is in place, which means the next twelve months are about depth rather than plumbing.

Job cards, end to end

Complete the PCB rollout so every board carries a full digital paper trail, then feed that process timing back into lead-time and capacity planning.

Inspection into production

Take the inspection pipeline from demonstrated to running on the line, with the assembly team, and start measuring it properly instead of projecting.

Machine data

The flow meters proved the pattern cheaply. Extending live machine data into the ERP is the logical next step, and the one that turns reporting into monitoring.

A deeper Vortex tier

A richer portal for our largest accounts, with more of the quality and delivery evidence they currently have to ask us for.

Not being the only engineer

One person is a risk to a platform this load-bearing. Documenting it properly, and eventually having someone alongside me, matters more than any single feature on this list.
