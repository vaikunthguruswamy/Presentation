What comes next

The plumbing is in place, so the next twelve months are about depth, not foundations. The direction is the one the wider sector calls Industry 4.0 — a connected factory joined by a single digital thread, from customer order to shop floor to dispatch. The difference is delivering it at a cost and pace a mid-size contract manufacturer can actually absorb.

In progress Next On the horizon
Digital thread · traceability In progress

Close the loop on job cards

Finish the PCB rollout so every board carries a full digital history from order to dispatch, then feed real process timings back into lead-time and capacity planning — replacing estimates with the factory's own record.

Machine vision QA Next

Inspection onto the line

Take the AI board-inspection pipeline from demonstrated to running alongside the assembly team — turning a day of manual program set-up into seconds — and measure the gain on the line instead of projecting it.

Industrial IoT · monitoring Next

Connect the machines

The £7 flow-meter sensors proved live plant data can be captured for a fraction of the £200+ off-the-shelf price. Extending that pattern to machine telemetry turns after-the-fact reporting into real-time monitoring, and eventually predictive maintenance.

Predictive analytics Next

Forecasting as standard

Generalise the prediction models already running on cost, wastage and throughput into one forecasting layer, so directors can see where a quarter is heading — not only where it has been.

Customer-facing digital On the horizon

A deeper Vortex tier

A richer portal for our largest accounts, surfacing the quality and delivery evidence they currently have to ask us for — the digital thread made visible to the customer.

Resilience & capability On the horizon

Beyond one engineer

A platform this load-bearing cannot rest on one person. Documenting it properly, and eventually a second engineer alongside me, matters more than any single feature on this list — it is how the transformation outlives its author.

None of this needs a big-bang investment. Each step reuses the same foundation, proves its value on one process, and only then scales — the same approach that got us here.
