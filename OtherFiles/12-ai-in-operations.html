Where AI is doing real work

Each of these sits inside a process someone was already doing by hand. None of them was adopted because it was interesting; every one removed a specific task. Where the data is sensitive, the models run on our own infrastructure.

Plain English to a chart

A manager describes what they want to see; a local model turns it into a query across the divisional databases and returns the data to plot. Dynamic questions get answered without waiting for me to build a dashboard.

Recruitment screening

Identifying details are removed first. Several agents then argue the candidate's strengths and weaknesses against the job description and converge on a score with a written rationale HR can challenge.

Document extraction

Supplier, utility and landfill invoices are read automatically into the ERP at around 94% extraction accuracy, feeding the environmental and cost dashboards without manual entry.

Bill of materials matching

Customers describe the same component in different ways. A fine-tuned model normalises each description, then the system searches stock and supplier catalogues and ranks candidates by how well they match.

Forecasting

Time-series models project sales trends and electricity, water and waste usage into the next month, quarter and financial year, so environmental and cost planning starts from a projection rather than last year's number.

Payroll assistant

HR can ask why a particular payslip looks the way it does. The assistant knows the payroll rules, decides which rules and records apply, and answers from the data rather than guessing.
