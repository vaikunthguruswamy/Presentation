What I found in February 2025

Each division ran its own order-management system on its own SQL Server, maintained for twenty to twenty-five years. Thousands of tables, hundreds of columns, no foreign keys, no documentation, and naming that did not agree between servers: the same field appears as no., prod_no and productNo.

Anything the systems did not cover was kept in spreadsheets. In the PCB division one workbook had been running since around 2005, on a machine that had not been switched off in years. It linked out to roughly a dozen more, each with twenty or thirty sheets, some ending in 2009, some in 2019, some starting in 2024, all tracking more or less the same thing in different formats. Thirty thousand rows, no schema, no backup, and no graph at the end of it.

Directors who wanted a view of performance exported from those systems by hand and rebuilt charts in Excel each time they needed them. The data existed. Nobody could see it.
