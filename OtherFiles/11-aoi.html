Inspecting assembled boards by comparison

New board designs arrive constantly, and each one needs its optical inspection programmed before it can be checked. The assembly team's first approach was a single end-to-end vision model, run locally for security. It was expensive to set up and slow in use, because full-resolution board images either overwhelmed the model or lost the detail that mattered when scaled down.

Working with the assembly engineer who raised it, I proposed treating it as an alignment and difference problem rather than a recognition problem. The reference board and the manufactured board are aligned, components are detected and masked on both, and only the differences are scored. It is robust to how the board is placed under the camera and to changes in lighting.

Being precise about status. The pipeline is designed to reduce the current inspection setup from days to seconds, and that is the figure in my first-round entry. It is not yet in full production use, so I would describe it as designed and demonstrated rather than measured. The judges should hold me to the difference.
